# Recorded Decisions

## Purpose

Every recorded decision in the graph, in one register, so that a decision id can be
resolved without grepping. Per `UF-6` in [Use Case Format](../overview/use-case-format.md) a page links the decisions
that bind it rather than embedding them.

## How an id is formed

A decision is cited as **`RD-<REGISTER>-<n>`**.

- **The register prefix is part of the id.** `RD-UR-6` and `RD-VRC-6` are different
  decisions. A bare `RD-6` resolves to nothing and must not be written.
- **The register names the subject the decision binds**, not the page that stores it -
  every decision below is stored here.
- **Numbers are historical.** They need not be contiguous within a register, and a
  number is not reused after its decision is removed.

| Register   | Subject                                                                                                               | Binds                                                                              |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-UR-*`  | Roles, the capability matrix, and their relationship to the personas                                                  | [User Roles](../product/user-roles.md)                                             |
| `RD-VRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate verification                                                                                     | [UC - Verify Restaurant Candidate](../use-cases/uc-verify-restaurant-candidate.md) |
| `RD-DRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate detection                                                                                        | [UC - Detect Restaurant Candidate](../use-cases/uc-detect-restaurant-candidate.md) |
| `RD-GL-*`  | The glossary's terms and the naming rule                                                                              | [Glossary](../product/glossary.md)                                                 |
| `RD-TS-*`  | Guest table sessions and table orders: occupancy, sharing, identity, expiry, the staff queue, and QR abuse protection | [Table Visit](../domain/table-visit.md)                                            |
| `RD-PM-*`  | Published restaurant menus: who may publish one, what a printed menu code names, and who is shown which page          | [UC - View Restaurant Menus](../use-cases/uc-view-restaurant-menus.md)             |

Decisions about **how the specification work is written and tracked** are not system
decisions and are deliberately not here: they are `RD-PD-*`, in
`claude/process-decisions.md` in the project docs.

| `RD-TS-1`  | **A guest scan does not occupy the table. It raises a pending signal that staff confirm.** A QR code establishes a table _context_ and never a presence: the sticker can be photographed, posted, or scanned from the car park. So a scan at a table nobody has seated writes a `pending` session, which cannot order, and the table's live state is not touched at all - the worst a remote scan can do is put one row on a screen in the restaurant. | `RD-TS-2`  | **Confirming is the seating, not a second action.** `transitionTableState` activates a table's pending sessions in the same commit that opens its visit, so a host who seats a party has already admitted every guest who scanned while waiting. A per-guest approval queue was considered and refused: it asks the restaurant a question it has already answered by sitting the party down, and it is work during service                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `RD-TS-3`  | **A table session is shared: the second guest to scan joins the first one's visit.** Not enforced by a check but by construction - the join path follows `TableState.visitId`, the single pointer at an open visit, written and dropped by the commit that moves the table's status. | `RD-TS-4`  | **A guest participates through anonymous authentication, and an anonymous account is not a member.** The uid is what `firestore.rules` matches the session document against and what `linkWith*` upgrades in place, so the guest who registers at the end of the meal keeps the session that knows what they ordered. | `RD-TS-7`  | **A menu is public information, served by a callable rather than by a read rule.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1102]. A public menu page needs both `/menus/{menuId}` and `/restaurants/{restaurantId}`, and opening the second in `firestore.rules` would publish `ownerUserId`, `claimStatus` and the whole `tableOrdering` configuration. `loadPublicMenu` assembles both halves field by field instead, which is what makes "the public read path exposes the menu only" hold as those documents grow. A menu with none renders bare numbers, which a reader can ask about, rather than the hardcoded euro sign the consumer renderer used to print at every restaurant on earth                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

No scheduled job exists, because a session nobody touches costs nothing and matters to nobody                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `RD-TS-9`  | **An order belongs to one phone; the visit is what is shared.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1103], settling the last unsettled half of the sharing question. One `TableOrder` per submission, under the visit, carrying the `guestUserId` and `sessionId` that sent it - so the party shares a visit and a bill, and "who ordered the second bottle" is answerable without asking every guest to have an account. A **per-line** attribution was considered and refused: a line is built from a cart row that already merges repeated taps, so attributing it would require the cart to stop merging, and a party ordering a round of the same beer would then read as one line per person. A single **table-wide** order was also refused: two phones ordering at once would contend on one document, and the restaurant would lose the grain the kitchen actually uses, which is the ticket                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `RD-TS-10` | **The guest's phone sends the prices it displayed, and any difference refuses the whole order.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1103]. There are three ways to keep "the prices on the order are the prices the guest saw" and only this one works. Snapshotting whatever the menu says at submission silently recharges a guest whose dish was repriced while they read its description; trusting the number the client sends lets a client name its own price. So the number is a **claim**, `submitTableOrder` compares it to the live menu, and a mismatch answers `priceChanged` naming the item and both prices. What is written is always the menu's number. The same argument runs for the currency and for availability, which is why all three refusals name the item. A **partial** order - send the lines that are still fine - was refused: three of the four dishes a table asked for is worse than none plus a sentence saying why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `RD-TS-11` | **The guest's order moves the table to `ordering`, and writes that transition itself.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1103]. `transitionTableState` is guarded by `requireTableStateAuthority`, which admits `staff`, `business` and `admin` and then checks `/restaurantStaff/{uid}` - a guest holds none of those, and widening the guard would hand the floor's state machine to anybody holding a photographed QR code. So `submitTableOrder` writes the `occupied -> ordering` change in its own transaction, from the same `TableState` and `TableStateTransition` types and against the same transition matrix; the types are the drift protection, because a field added to either is a compile error in both places. A table **already** `ordering` is left alone and the order still lands, since refusing that would refuse every round after the first                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `RD-TS-12` | **A guest's status screen shows their own orders, not the party's.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1104]. `RD-TS-9` shares the _visit_ and the bill, so the opposite answer was the obvious one and was refused on what a phone can actually prove. A visit-scoped read needs the guest's membership of that visit provable in `firestore.rules`, and the only pointer at it is the session document - whose name is derived from the table the guest **scanned**, while the visit names the table the party is sitting at **now**. | `RD-TS-13` | **The cancellation reason is declared with its reader and written by its writer.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1104]. `TableOrder.cancellationReason` is added by the issue that **renders** it, and filled by the staff cancellation of issue [#1105]. That is the reverse of the order this repository usually works in, and it is the reverse of `RD-TS-9`'s treatment of `idempotencyKey`, which [#1103] deliberately left absent for [#1108] to add - the distinction being who the field is _for_. An idempotency key is a client's to mint and would have to be unlearned; a cancellation reason exists only so that a guest is told why, so a queue that invented its own shape would be recording sentences into a field no screen reads. It is optional, and an absent one is ordinary rather than a gap: a kitchen mid-rush is not always going to type a sentence, so the screen names the cancellation either way and adds the reason where there is one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `RD-TS-14` | **The staff queue reads one collection-group query, scoped to the restaurant by a field on the order.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1105]. Orders hang from the visit (`RD-TS-9`), so tonight's orders are spread over one subcollection per party and there is no path that holds them all. The alternative was a Firestore listener per open visit, attached and detached on every seating and every clearing - a listener churn proportional to the service rather than to the room. So the queue is `collectionGroup('orders')` filtered on `restaurantId`, which `submitTableOrder` already writes on to every order. The rule that admits it has no `{restaurantId}` in its path and therefore reads `resource.data.restaurantId`, which makes the `where` the **permission** rather than a filter: Firestore admits the query only when its constraints prove the condition, so a query without it is refused whole rather than returning every order in BiteTribe. That is `RD-TS-12`'s mechanism pointed at a restaurant instead of at a guest. A second `where` on the status bounds the read to the open orders, because a queue that streamed every order a restaurant had ever taken would grow without limit. Both need a collection-group index exemption, and indexes in this repository deployed by hand when this was taken - they deploy from CI on every push to `develop` since [#1567]                                                                                                               |
| `RD-TS-15` | **A queue row waits for the backend; the floor plan does not.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1105], and deliberately the opposite of the optimistic layer `RD`-less issue [#1094] built for a table. So the row shows that it is busy and waits - per order, so one slow call does not freeze the rest of the pass. | `RD-TS-18` | **A call for a waiter is named after the table and the kind, not generated.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1106]. | `RD-TS-20` | **The rate limit is the document name plus one clock, and the clock runs from the request.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1106]. | `RD-TS-22` | **An acknowledgement has no `expectedStatus`, and a second press is not a conflict.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1106], and the one place this deliberately differs from `transitionTableOrderStatus` beside it. An order transition has four destinations and two members of staff pressing different ones in the same second is a real disagreement worth refusing; an acknowledgement has exactly one destination, so two people pressing it both wanted what happened and the row has already gone from both screens. | `RD-TS-23` | **The two staff lists sort in opposite directions, on one screen, on purpose.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue [#1106]. The repeat tap is what makes that stable: `lastRequestedAt` moves and `requestedAt` does not, so tapping again cannot push a table up the list - the row says "asked again" instead. The urgency threshold differs for the same reason: three minutes for a raised hand against twelve for an order, because an order is waiting on a kitchen that is cooking it and a raised hand is waiting on nobody at all                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `RD-TS-24` | **The scan limit is two counters, cheapest first, and the durable one is the higher.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1107]. The in-memory counter of issue [#1100] stays in front of the durable one in `durable-scan-rate-limit.ts`, and the order is the whole cost argument: a flood against one warm instance is absorbed for free after the thirtieth request - no reads, no writes, nothing that grows with how hard somebody is trying - and only traffic spread thinly enough to look ordinary to each instance reaches counters that cost something. The durable limits are therefore deliberately **higher** than the per-instance ones rather than a second opinion on the same requests: they count the aggregate across however many instances are running, and setting them at or below the memory limits would mean the free gate never fired. A third dimension is added, the IP, because `clientOf` answers the uid **or** the address - so an attacker holding one throwaway anonymous account per request is bucketed by neither, and anonymous accounts are free and unlimited by `RD-TS-4`. What that dimension is worth is stated rather than assumed: `X-Forwarded-For` reaches `req.ip`, so it is a cost raiser and not a boundary, and the token and client counts are the two a caller cannot choose. A Firestore failure **admits** the scan, because a limiter that cannot reach its counters and therefore refuses every guest in the product is a worse outage than the one it was reacting to |
| `RD-TS-25` | **An anomaly is a table signal, not a log, and it is raised on the crossing rather than on the refusal.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1107]. `/restaurants/{id}/scanAnomalies/{n}_{tableId}_{kind}` takes `RD-TS-18`'s address for `RD-TS-18`'s three reasons: the collection is bounded by the room at one document per table per kind, a repeat joins the row that is up, and a staff screen reads the whole of it with no query, no index and no collection-group rule. The log this replaces would have grown **with the attack**, which is a denial of service wearing the costume of a security feature. The same reasoning bounds the writer twice over: only the request that takes a bucket past its limit raises a row, and a row already raised inside `SCAN_ANOMALY_QUIET_MS` is not written again - so `ScanAnomaly.count` counts **raisings and not requests**, which the row says, and forty thousand resolutions in a minute are one. Nothing reads an anomaly back to decide anything, so a wrong one costs a restaurant a dismissed row rather than a guest a meal, and a dismissal re-opens on the next raising because it says somebody read the row and not that the table is exempt                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `RD-TS-26` | **A pending session is drawn with the staff screens, and is deliberately not an anomaly.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1107], answering the question that issue put to whoever picked it up. `RD-TS-1` says a scan at an unseated table raises a signal staff confirm; issue [#1101] wrote that signal, `firestore.rules` admitted every reader of the floor plan to it, and nothing drew it - so the confirming was asked of a screen that did not show it, and issue [#1106] refused a guest's call for a waiter from a `pending` session while naming this as the reason. It lands as its own list above the tables that are calling, in the warning tone rather than the danger one, grouped one row per **table** rather than per phone - three friends who each scan the code at the door are one party, and three rows would ask a host to seat one table three times. Folding it into the anomaly list would have made the ordinary case look like an incident, which is the reliable way to get a list stopped being read; a guest at the door is a person waiting, and the answer to them is to walk over                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `RD-TS-27` | **The coarse location is a signal on a screen and never a gate, and the coordinates are not stored.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1107]. A guest may tick a box on the confirmation screen to share roughly where they are; a guest who does not, whose device has no fix, or whose app has no location grant starts exactly the same session, reads the same menu and places the same order. That is what "never as the sole gate" has to mean in code rather than in prose - a check that is optional but denies something when present is a gate with an opt-out, and the people it would lock out are a basement dining room and an old phone rather than anybody photographing stickers. It **never prompts**: `getCurrentPosition` refuses rather than asking when the grant is undetermined, because the OS prompt belongs to onboarding, which explains what a position is for before spending it. The coordinates are compared to the restaurant's and discarded, and only a rounded distance survives, on the rows far enough away to be worth one - a document holding a guest's latitude would be readable by every member of staff through `readsFloorPlan`, which is a reasonable rule for "table 12 is calling" and an unreasonable one for where somebody was standing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `RD-TS-28` | **Rotation is the restaurant's authority; clearing the row that prompts it is the floor's.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1107]. `dismissScanAnomaly` takes `requireTableStateAuthority`, the same door as seating a table and answering a call, because the rows sit on the screens the floor is already being read from and a second list of who may clear one would be free to disagree with who may answer a table. `rotateTableQrToken` and the new `rotateTableQrTokens` keep `requireRestaurantAuthority`: every other action on those screens moves something on a floor and is undone by moving it back, and this one invalidates a printed sticker and is undone by a trip to the printer. The bulk rotation refuses a call that names **neither** a room nor a set of tables, because "rotate every code in the building" must not be what a caller gets by leaving an argument out; it runs one transaction per table rather than one over the room, because the all-or-nothing guarantee cannot honestly be had at that size and an owner whose run failed at table 19 of 24 is better served by 18 rotated tables and an error than by nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `RD-TS-29` | **An order's idempotency key names its document, and the phone can derive that name.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108]. `submitTableOrder` writes an order at `visits/{visitId}/orders/req-{requestId}` when the caller sends a key, which turns deduplication into a read of one document rather than a search for an order that looks similar: the replay reads the id it would write, and two copies of one request racing each other contend on it because the read is inside the transaction. The prefix and the 8-to-128 character pattern are `RD`-less inheritances from issue [#1096]'s table transitions, for the two reasons that issue gives - the key has to be a legal Firestore document name, and it has to be one no auto-generated id could ever be, or a client could hand in a twenty-character alphanumeric string that happens to name somebody else's order and be answered with their dinner. What is new here is that `tableOrderDocumentId` lives in the **model** rather than only in the backend, because the guest's phone derives the same name to recognise its own order in the list it is already listening to                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `RD-TS-30` | **A replay is answered before the meal it happened in is looked at.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108]. The order document is read straight after the session, and an existing one is returned before the session status, the visit, the table status and every line are checked. A replay arrives after the world has moved on - the kitchen may have paused, the party may have been moved, the guest's own session may have been closed by staff - and none of that makes the order that already landed untrue. The one thing it must not do is land twice. The answer carries `replayed: true`, because the two are the same outcome and not the same event: a phone that gave up and retried is shown "this was already with the kitchen" rather than a second confirmation of an order placed once                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `RD-TS-31` | **The phone tells "it never left" apart from "we cannot say", and never invents either.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108]. A submission that could not be resolved reports `notSent` when no attempt ever left the device - the radio was off for every one of them - and `unknown` when a call went out and came back with nothing. The distinction is the issue's "a guest is never left unsure whether their order was placed" read literally: inventing a doubt where there is none teaches a guest to order twice to be sure, and inventing a certainty where there is a doubt means the dish turns up anyway. Both are offered the same way out, because the key makes the retry safe either way                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `RD-TS-32` | **An unresolved submission freezes the cart rather than replacing the screen.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108]. The refusal design of issue [#1103] keeps the menu and the cart on screen, and this keeps them for the stronger reason - the guest has to see what they sent. What it takes away is the _editing_: while a submission is unresolved the cart cannot be changed, because the order it describes may already be with the kitchen, and a cart that accepted an edit would build a round that can never be sent - the key already names an order without it. You cannot change an order you might already have placed, and the way out is one tap that either confirms it or releases the cart                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `RD-TS-33` | **The cart is kept on the phone and rebuilt from the live menu.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108], revisiting issue [#1103]'s "the cart is never stored". That rule was about who pays for a guest changing their mind, and the answer is still nobody: the cart never reaches Firestore and costs the restaurant no write per tap. It is kept in device storage, keyed by restaurant and table rather than by the guest's account, because a phone is one guest and an anonymous uid the app re-minted is not something a guest chose or could be asked about. What is stored is **ids**, never dishes: a row is rebuilt from the menu on screen now, so a restored cart cannot carry a dish that has been taken off, marked unavailable or left unpriced, and a repriced dish comes back at the price the guest can see. Storing the item itself would be keeping a second, stale menu on the phone and then ordering from the copy                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `RD-TS-34` | **A written-down submission is resolved unprompted within a meal, and dropped after one.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue [#1108]. The screen that finds a record on load sends it again rather than waiting for the guest to wonder, because sending it again _is_ the question - the backend answers a key it has already seen with the order it wrote, so one request either reconciles the phone with the truth or places the order nobody ever answered. What bounds it is the record's age: one older than `PENDING_ORDER_MAX_AGE_MS` is dropped without being sent, since the restaurant's own session idle timeout has closed the table by then and an order arriving in a dining room the guest left hours ago is worse than a record nobody is watching for                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

| `RD-TS-35` | **An extra belongs to a category, and applies to every dish in it.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1598], which had to settle this before it could build anything: `Category.extrasBlock` had carried extras since before stage 3 with nothing rendering them, and "extra cheese" reads as a property of a pizza rather than of the Pizze section. It stays on the category, and the block's own `description` is the argument - it is the owner's sentence saying who the extras are offered with, "add to any pizza", which is what a paper menu prints once under a section rather than beside each dish on it. Moving the block to the item would make an owner retype and re-price one extra on every pizza, and a menu maintained that way disagrees with itself within a week. The rule is applied in one place, `menuExtrasForItem`, so the cart, both renderers and the backend's revalidation cannot offer or refuse different things; a variant takes its dish's extras, because a large Margherita is a Margherita. What the shape cannot say is that an extra is offered on _some_ items of a category, which is the same class as the cardinality rules the issue puts out of scope and is reachable later as an allowlist on the item without moving the data |
| `RD-TS-36` | **An extra is revalidated and snapshotted on exactly the terms a dish is.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1598]. An order line records each extra it was ordered with by id, name and price, because an owner who reprices extra mozzarella on Tuesday must not change what Monday's receipt says it cost - the argument `OrderLineSnapshot` already makes for the dish, one row further in. The dish's `price` stays the dish's and the extras are their own field: folding them into it would make the backend compare a sum against a menu price and refuse every order that carried an extra, so what one unit costs is `orderLineUnitPrice` instead. The refusals are their own two reasons rather than `itemMissing` and `priceChanged`, because they call for something different from the guest - a dish that is gone means picking something else, an extra that is gone means the dish is still there without it. A line naming the same extra twice is an `invalid-argument` and not a refusal: an extra is a tick box rather than a quantity, so a duplicate is a client bug no screen can produce, and a refusal would tell a guest to fix something they never did |
| `RD-TS-37` | **An anonymous table session reads its own table and nothing else of BiteTribe.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1565]. `signedIn()` is `request.auth != null` and nothing in `firestore.rules` reads the sign-in provider, so from issue [#1101] onwards a uid minted by scanning a sticker satisfied every read clause written with a member in mind - `/users` and its follow graph, `/bites` and their likes, `/reviews`, `/bucketlists`, `/biteTrails` with `sells` and `ratings`, `/restaurants`, `/menus`, `/meta`, `/displayNames` and `/restaurantCandidates`, fifteen clauses - and left no `/users` document to trace the reader by. The fix is a second predicate rather than fifteen rewrites: `isMember()` is `signedIn()` and a sign-in provider that is not `anonymous`, and the general read clauses ask it instead. The guest keeps every clause written _for_ it, because those match its uid rather than its kind: its own session, its own orders, the assistance request it raised, the floor-plan documents the table screens need. What this does **not** do is narrow what one member reads about another - `/users` stays readable by any member on purpose, and issue [#1611] is already narrowing the leaderboard half of that surface |
| `RD-TS-38` | **A callable that carries a uid is not therefore a callable a table guest may call.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1565]. `callable-authorization.spec.ts` classified 23 callables as `authenticated`, which means "carries a uid" and nothing more; four are the table callables and the other nineteen are member work, among them `getPlaceDetails`, `searchPlaces`, `searchNearbyPlaces` and `getCurrencyByPosition`, which spend billed Google Maps quota, and `deleteOwnAccount`, `claimDisplayName` and `updateUserMetadata`, which act on an account nobody behind an anonymous session has. `authenticated` therefore splits: `member` refuses a token whose sign-in provider is `anonymous`, and `anySession` is exactly `startTableSession`, `leaveTableSession`, `submitTableOrder` and `requestTableAssistance`. The spec table is the enforcement point as well as the record, so a twentieth callable has to name which of the two it is rather than joining the permissive one by default - which is how the first nineteen got there |
| `RD-TS-39` | **Revoking a table code stops the next scan and never the meal in progress.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1565], settling the half of its original criteria that asked for the opposite. `resolveTableQrToken` refuses a `revoked` or `superseded` token the moment it is written, so no new session starts on a code that was withdrawn; the sessions the code already minted run until the visit closes or the idle timeout fires, and `submitTableOrder` deliberately re-reads the restaurant, the session and the visit but never the token. The party this protects is the ordinary one: a sheet is reprinted because it was spilled on, and ending live sessions would log out four phones mid-main-course and make a waiter reseat a table that is perfectly fine. The remedy for a party that should _not_ be there is the one the floor already has - closing the visit, which `syncTableSessions` ends every session of in the same write |
| `RD-TS-40` | **The guest boundary is read off the token, and the link is what refreshes it.** Taken 20 September 2026 on issue [#1565]. The durable alternative - `exists(/users/$(uid))`, which is true for a member because `createUserOnAuthCreate` wrote the document and false for a guest because it skips one - is immune to a stale token and costs a document read on every evaluation of fifteen read clauses, billed, and paid partly by the guest's own cheap reads. `request.auth.token.firebase.sign_in_provider` costs nothing and is already how `start-table-session.ts` answers the same question. Its cost is a window: a session that issue [#1657] links to a real account may keep reporting `anonymous` until the token refreshes, and a member who reads as a guest gets empty lists rather than an error, which is the kind of bug that is reported a week late as "the feed is broken". So the link forces a token refresh, and the emulator test that a just-linked session reads the feed belongs to [#1657] rather than here |

## `RD-PM-*` - Published restaurant menus

Taken 19 September 2026, on issue [#370], which delivered issue [#371]. They are separated
from `RD-TS-*` because a published menu turned out not to be a table feature: issue [#1102]
built it under the table epic because that is where the need first appeared, and [#370]
established that it needs no table, no floor plan and no ordering.

| #         | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-PM-1` | **A menu code names a restaurant, and is produced on the restaurant's own page.** Not in the floor-plan editor, and not from the printable table sheets of issue [#1087], both of which would have made publishing a menu conditional on drawing a floor plan and defining tables. The cheapest thing a restaurant can do with BiteTribe is put its menu behind a sticker in the window, and that must ask nothing of it but a menu                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-PM-2` | **A table code and a menu code are different printed things, drawn by one shared encoder.** The encoder and its SVG renderer moved to `common/ui/qr-code`; the table platform kept only its own payload - the scan URL and the alphanumeric split of its token. A menu code that imported from the floor-plan library would re-create the coupling `RD-PM-1` removes, and a second encoder is how two printed codes start disagreeing about a quiet zone                                                                                                                    |
| `RD-PM-3` | **A printed code's text is derived from what the code encodes, never passed beside it.** The shared encoder builds the URL it prints from the segments it encoded. The alternative - handing it both - permits a sheet whose printed address and scannable code disagree, and that disagreement is discovered on a sticker already glued to a table                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-PM-4` | **A published menu address is not a secret and is not rotated.** It is the restaurant's id. The table tokens of issue [#1107] are opaque, rotatable and revocable because they scope a table and authorise a session; a menu address authorises nothing and leads to a page that cannot order. Giving it rotation would be protecting a menu from being read                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `RD-PM-5` | **Only a restaurant a business account holds may publish a menu under its name.** `ownerUserId` set and `claimStatus` not revoked, the same test the scan applies, and it survives the absence of ordering for a different reason: an unheld restaurant's menu is whatever was derived from other people's Bites, so publishing it under the restaurant's name states prices nobody there ever confirmed. The cost is accepted knowingly - almost nothing in production is held, so the feature waits on claiming rather than on itself                                     |
| `RD-PM-6` | **One printed address, and the route decides which page answers it.** A member with the app is handed the app's own menu page, with its chrome and the button that turns a dish into a Bite; everybody else - an anonymous table guest included - reads the published page. Printing two addresses, or asking the reader which they are, would put a question in front of a menu. The menu is resolved **before** that redirect, so a restaurant that printed a code and has no menu settles on the published page's refusal rather than on a route that would send it back |
| `RD-PM-7` | **The app is offered under the menu, never in front of it.** The published page is the one surface where somebody who has never heard of BiteTribe is holding a BiteTribe page, so the offer belongs there - but an interstitial in front of a restaurant's menu is exactly the wall the address exists to remove. It renders nothing inside the native wrapper, because offering the app to somebody reading this inside the app is noise                                                                                                                                  |

| `RD-VRC-6`  | **Scope.** `V19` and `V23` stay inside the verification transaction but are _referenced_ steps, owned by `UC-GIM` and `UC-MBR`. This Use Case covers verification only; dismissal and duplicate resolution are referenced Use Cases with their own terminal states. Owner assignment was an optional step at `V10`, referencing `UC-ARO`. **Corrected 9 September 2026:** [#1077] delivered it as a separate Operator action in the Admin App, against a Restaurant that already exists and a target that already holds `business`. `V10` is removed; `UC-ARO` is downstream of `END-V4` rather than a step inside this Use Case (`R-15`)                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-VRC-7`  | **`merged` is removed.** `merged` and `mergedIntoCandidateId` go from both model copies, and `V17`'s merge branch with them. A duplicate Candidate is resolved against an **already verified Restaurant**, which needs no status of its own. Real merging already happens by document-id convergence, which unions `biteIds` without any status, event or audit trail                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `RD-VRC-8`  | **`UC-DRC` owns every producer.** Both the automatic trigger and the Operator's on-demand clustering callable belong to detection, so the threshold divergence is stated once, next to the threshold                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `RD-VRC-9`  | **Refused evidence is not rerouted.** When a write onto a non-`pending` Candidate is refused, the refusal is logged and no Bite is written. Those Bites carry no `restaurantId` and therefore stay visible on the Admin App's Bite-places surface. Detection does not become a writer of `bite.restaurantId`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `RD-VRC-10` | **The on-demand producer keeps no evidence threshold.** A deliberate Operator action is the safeguard. The collection's invariant is restated instead: every Candidate is either backed by at least five matching Bites within 200 m, **or** marked as Operator-created                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `RD-VRC-11` | **The derived-menu marker never clears automatically.** Retraction is an explicit confirmation by the Restaurant Owner, and is sequenced behind owner assignment (`UC-ARO`) rather than shipped alongside the marker — so the marker is never a declared state nothing can reach, which is the mistake `RD-VRC-7` removes. **Premise corrected 9 September 2026:** the sequencing was originally justified by `ownerUserId` having no writer, and [#1077] gave it one. **Corrected again 10 September 2026:** [#1079] scoped the Business App to the assigned restaurants, so the surface a Restaurant Owner would retract from exists. The decision stands on the half that remains — nothing is assigned in production, so there is still no Restaurant Owner who could retract |
| `RD-VRC-16` | **The Candidate list has one server-side order and no selectable ordering:** `evidence.biteCount` descending, then `createdAtTimestamp` descending. The consumer app's client-side sort criterion reorders an already-fetched array, which cannot affect _which_ Candidates a limited query returns — and that is this list's actual defect. A work queue for two Operators also has one defensible default, unlike a discovery feed where preference genuinely varies. Recorded so the divergence from the consumer convention reads as deliberate                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

## `RD-DRC-*` - Restaurant candidate detection

Taken 7 September 2026.

| #           | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-DRC-18` | **Detection is one Use Case with two entries and one shared kernel,** not two Use Cases. Entry A has no human actor and Entry B is Operator work, which is the argument for splitting; the argument that wins is that both execute the same clustering code, so `R-1`, `R-5`, `R-7`, `R-8`, `R-9` and `R-10` would have to be stated twice and kept in agreement by hand. `K5` is the only step whose behaviour differs by entry, which makes `RD-VRC-10` one gated step instead of a fact duplicated across two pages                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `RD-DRC-19` | **Automatic detection is a mechanism, not an entry.** It has no actor: it runs in the Functions runtime, is callable from no client, and no human is in the loop. `AF-4` reserves the word _actogram_ for a flow containing at least one interaction step, and names a flow without one a mechanism.
