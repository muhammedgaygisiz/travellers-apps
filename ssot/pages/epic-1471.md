- [epic: grow bite-tribe-admin into the BiteTribe operations tool](https://github.com/muhammedgaygisiz/travellers-apps/issues/1471) (Issue \#1471)
- Description
  - \# Epic: grow bite-tribe-admin into the BiteTribe operations tool
  - The admin app exists. Issue \#1469 created it, deployed it, gated both privileged apps on Firebase Auth custom claims, and built user management as its first operator surface. This epic owns the basic day-to-day operations that make it usable: managing accounts, blocking an abusive user, and removing content that should not be there.
  - \#\# Why this is separate
  - \#1469 answers one question: does an operator-only app exist and can only operators reach it. It does not answer what operations happen in it, and the two need different decisions.
  - Three things are true today and none of them is acceptable for long:
  - **BiteTribe-internal operations still live in the app we hand to restaurants.** The `migrations` page and restaurant-candidate verification sit in `bite-tribe-business`. They are gated on the `business` role, which every restaurant will hold. A restaurant can run our collection-wide migrations and verify restaurant candidates.
  - **The privileged callables do not check who is calling.** `verifyRestaurantCandidate` accepts any authenticated caller, and it is the callable that creates restaurants. `setUserRoles` and `listUsersWithRoles` require `admin` because they were written after the role existed; nothing older does.
  - **There is no way to act on abuse.** A Bite that should not exist cannot be removed by anyone but its author. An abusive account cannot be stopped. Both require console access and manual Firestore edits today.
  - \#\# Goal
  - An operator can run BiteTribe's day-to-day operations from the admin app: find an account, change what it may do, stop it entirely, and remove content that should not be there. The business app contains only what a restaurant does to its own data.
  - \#\# Scope
  - \#\#\# Harden every privileged callable
  - Audit each callable in `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/functions/src/functions` for who may call it, and apply `requireAdmin` where the answer is "an operator".
  - `verifyRestaurantCandidate` is the known case. It is not necessarily the only one.
  - Independent of \#1078: rules protect documents, `requireAdmin` protects operations.
  - \#\#\# Move the operator surfaces out of the business app
  - `migrations` moves to the admin app. `UC - Run Operational Migrations` describes it as running from "business tooling", which stops being true.
  - Restaurant-candidate verification moves with it, including the dashboard list that feeds it.
  - The business app keeps only what a restaurant does to its own data.
  - \#\#\# Block an account
  - Blocking sets Firebase Auth's `disabled` flag, so the account cannot sign in at all. Firebase enforces it; nothing needs to be written into rules.
  - Blocking and content removal stay separate actions. An operator who wants both does both, and the log shows two entries.
  - \#\#\# Remove an improper Bite
  - An operator can delete a Bite outright. It is not hidden or tombstoned: it is gone.
  - Deletion has to leave the derived state correct — the Bite counters, likes, reviews, the image in Storage, and any restaurant candidate that references it.
  - \#\#\# See and change a subscription tier
  - An operator can read an account's tier and set it to Free or Pro for a support case, with a reason that lands in the log.
  - `subscriptionTier` is a display mirror today, not an entitlement: nothing in the product gates on it. The operator surface is still this epic's, because it is the same guard, callable and log shape as every other operator action.
  - What the tier eventually _enforces_ is not here. \#1122 and \#1126 own the entitlement, the claim and the gates.
  - \#\#\# Find the account or the Bite to act on
  - Reuse the existing `searchUsers` and `searchBites` callables rather than building a second search.
  - The **UI** cannot be reused: `libs/bite-tribe/search/*` is `scope:bite-tribe`, which `scope:bite-tribe-admin` may not import. The callables are the reusable part.
  - \#\#\# Keep the operator log readable
  - The audit trail is Cloud Logging, deliberately: there is no in-app record and none is planned at this scale.
  - That only works if every operator action logs the same way. `setUserRoles` already logs actor, target and outcome; every new operator callable follows the same shape so one query answers "what did we do to this account".
  - \#1477 made that shape one helper, `logOperatorAction`, with a spec that fails the build when a callable logs its own way. The fields, the queries and the retention window the whole decision rests on are in [[Architecture - Auth]].
  - \#\# Success criteria
  - No BiteTribe-internal operation is reachable from `bite-tribe-business`.
  - Every callable that performs an operator action rejects a caller without `admin`, proven by a test per callable.
  - An operator can find an account, block it, and see that it can no longer sign in.
  - An operator can find a Bite and delete it, and nothing downstream is left referring to it.
  - An operator can see an account's subscription tier and change it, and the change is recorded with actor, target and reason.
  - One Cloud Logging query answers what operators did to a given account or Bite.
  - The consumer app is unaffected by every change in this epic.
  - \#\# Out of scope
  - **The restaurant claim flow.** Verifying a claim, assigning an owner and granting the business role belong to \#1069 and \#1077, not here. This epic is about operations on accounts and content.
  - Replacing the open Firestore rules (\#1078), which keeps its own branch, emulator pass and rollback plan.
  - An in-app audit trail. Cloud Logging is the record by decision, not by omission.
  - Hiding or soft-deleting content, and write-bans that let a blocked user keep reading. Both were considered and rejected for this pass: removal is a delete, blocking is a sign-in block.
  - Automated or model-assisted moderation, and the report queue itself. \#1284 owns those; this epic gives an operator the actions a report would eventually trigger.
  - Splitting `admin` into narrower roles. Two operators today, one role.
  - **Billing itself.** Store purchases, RevenueCat, webhooks, renewals and refunds belong to \#1124; the entitlement record, the custom claim and the gates a tier will one day enforce belong to \#1122 and \#1126. This epic owns the operator surface over the tier, not what the tier means.
  - \#\# Product decisions to confirm
  - **What stops an already-signed-in blocked user?** Firebase honours `disabled` at token refresh, so a live session survives up to an hour. Proposal: call `revokeRefreshTokens` alongside disabling, and accept that an ID token already issued stays valid until it expires.
  - **Does deleting a Bite need a reason?** Nothing enforces one, and the log is the only record. Proposal: require a short reason on the callable so the log entry is worth reading later.
  - **Can an operator block another operator?** Nothing prevents it. `setUserRoles` already refuses to let an admin drop their own admin role; blocking has the same lockout shape.
  - **What does an operator see of a private profile?** `searchUsers` respects the public flag today. An operator acting on abuse probably should not.

- Notes
  - The epic was scoped down after it was filed. It first covered the restaurant claim flow; that belongs to \#1069 and \#1077, and this epic is about operations on accounts and content instead. The claim flow is what motivated the admin app in the first place, so its absence here is a decision rather than an oversight.
  - Two of its scope items are corrections rather than new work, and both predate the admin app. `migrations` and restaurant-candidate verification are BiteTribe-internal operations living in the business app, and `verifyRestaurantCandidate` has always accepted any authenticated caller.
  - Three product decisions were taken when the child issues were filed, and each closed off a cheaper-looking option. Content removal is a **delete**, not a hide, accepting that a mistake is unrecoverable, because hiding costs every read path. Blocking is Firebase Auth `disabled` and nothing else, accepting that a live session survives up to an hour, because a write-ban needs rules that do not exist. The audit trail is Cloud Logging with no in-app surface, accepting that reading it needs project access.
  - Subscription administration was filed as out of scope when the epic was written, and was brought back in on 6 September 2026 as \#1485. The exclusion was about billing, and reading it as covering the operator surface too left the one account attribute an operator most often has to change with no path but the Firebase console. What stays out is unchanged: this epic writes the mirror, \#1122 and \#1126 decide what it enforces.
  - \#1284 was written before an admin role existed. It asks "who is an operator?" and proposes putting its report queue on the business `migrations` page. Both answers changed with \#1469, and that epic should be re-read rather than followed as written.
