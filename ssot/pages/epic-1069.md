- [epic: Restaurant ownership, claiming and authorization](https://github.com/muhammedgaygisiz/travellers-apps/issues/1069) (Issue \#1069)
- Description
  - \# Epic: Restaurant ownership, claiming and authorization
  - Part of \#735 (stage 0).
  - Give restaurants a real owner and give the backend a real authorization model, so that everything built on top of the floor plan can be trusted.
  - \#\# Why this is first
  - The Restaurant Interaction Platform vision assumes restaurants can be claimed. They cannot today.
  - `Restaurant` has no owner or claim state. **Corrected 30 August 2026:** \#1074 added `ownerUserId`, `claimStatus`, `claimedAt` and `claimedAtTimestamp` as optional fields. ~~Nothing writes them.~~ **Corrected again 8 September 2026:** \#1077 is the writer and is done - `assignRestaurantOwner` and `revokeRestaurantOwner`, with an operator surface in the admin app. ~~Nothing _reads_ the field yet; \#1079 is the reader and \#1078 is the enforcement.~~ **Both are done:** \#1078 enforces the field in the Firestore rules, and \#1079 reads it in the business app's restaurant query and in the guard on its two edit routes.
  - No roles, custom claims, or membership checks exist in `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/functions/src`. **Corrected 8 September 2026: no longer true.** \#1469 delivered `admin` and `business` as Firebase Auth custom claims with `setUserRoles` as the only writer, and \#1472 put `requireAdmin` on every operator callable with a build-failing endpoint classification. What is still missing is the `staff` role and any backend guard for `business`, which \#1075 now owns.
  - `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/firestore.rules` allows every authenticated user to read and write every document. ~~**Still true**, and the reason this epic is not done: \#1078 is unstarted.~~ **No longer true:** \#1078 replaced the blanket match with ownership-scoped rules. Reads stayed where they were, so what a business account _sees_ is scoped by \#1079's client query rather than by the rules.
  - Any floor plan, table state, visit, or order written under those rules is writable by any logged-in user. Building stages 1 to 4 first would mean shipping a restaurant operations system with no access control and retrofitting it later, which is strictly harder and riskier.
  - \#\# Goal
  - A restaurant has exactly one accountable owner: a normal user carrying the `business` role. Business users can only read and write the restaurants assigned to them. Staff can act on those restaurants within a narrower permission set. Operators assign and revoke ownership.
  - \#\# Scope
  - Ownership and claim fields on the Restaurant model
  - The `admin`, `business` and `staff` roles as verifiable backend identity, not client-side state
  - An operator assigning and revoking restaurant ownership in the admin app
  - A business account managing the staff on its own restaurant
  - Ownership-scoped Firestore security rules replacing the current open rules
  - Business dashboard scoped to the assigned restaurants
  - \#\# Success criteria
  - A restaurant cannot be edited by a user who does not own it, proven with emulator rule tests for both allow and deny.
  - The Firestore rules no longer contain a blanket `allow read, write: if request.auth != null` for all documents.
  - An operator can assign a verified restaurant to a business account, and revoke it, and the effect is visible in the business app without a redeploy.
  - A business account can add and remove the staff on a restaurant it holds, and cannot touch a restaurant it does not.
  - Existing unowned restaurants keep working for read paths in the consumer app and are not orphaned by the migration.
  - Roles are carried in verified backend identity so they cannot be forged from the client.
  - \#\# Out of scope
  - Billing, subscriptions, or paid business tiers
  - Shared restaurant ownership (one owner per restaurant for now)
  - Self-service restaurant claiming by a business user (\#1076, closed as not planned)
  - Automated ownership verification via external registries or phone call-back
  - Consumer-side UI changes beyond what the rules require
  - \#\# Product decisions to confirm
  - Settled 8 September 2026.
  - ~~Is ownership held by a user or by an organisation? Proposal: by organisation, with the claiming user as the first member, because `users` and `biteTrails` already carry `organisationId`.~~ **A user carrying the `business` role.** The premise was wrong: nothing ever wrote `organisationId`, the organisation dashboard that listed employees by it was permanently empty, and \#1371 removed the fields.
  - ~~What evidence is required to approve a claim? Proposal: manual admin review for the first iteration, since volume is low pre-scale.~~ **There is no claim to approve.** An operator assigns the restaurant directly in the admin app; verification happens off-system, on the call the operator is already having. That is the manual-review proposal with the queue removed, so \#1076 is closed as not planned and \#1077 is rewritten as the assignment workflow.
  - **What happens to Bites and menus of a restaurant when ownership changes?** Nothing moves; ownership grants maintenance rights only. Confirmed as proposed.
  - **How do the three roles relate?** `admin`, `business` and `staff` are independent. A staff account holds `staff` and not `business`, so `roleGuard` has to accept a set of roles rather than one. Holding `business` and `staff` together is contradictory and refused. ~~Provisional: it depends on the assignment mechanics \#1077 settles.~~ **Settled by \#1077:** ownership is a field on the restaurant document, so nothing about the assignment needs `staff` to be a narrowing of `business`.
  - **What does a business account see on the day \#1079 deploys?** The restaurants assigned to it, and nothing else. Settled 8 September 2026. Falling back to the unowned restaurants would spare every existing business account from losing its list, and was refused: \#1078's rules would immediately contradict it, and the point of the epic is a boundary rather than a boundary with an exception. Nothing in production is assigned today, so the existing restaurants have to be assigned by an operator before or alongside \#1079.
  - **Where does "which restaurants is this account assigned to" live?** In Firestore, never in a custom claim. An assignment change then takes effect immediately rather than after up to an hour, there is no 1000-byte claim cap to grow into, and \#1078's rules read the same field — a claim copy would be a second version of one fact that can disagree with it.

- Notes
  - The epic was rescoped on 8 September 2026, and the change is bigger than its child list suggests. It was specified as a self-service claim flow: a restaurant asks for itself, an operator reviews the request, and a `RestaurantClaim` document carries the evidence and the contested states. Ownership is assigned **directly by an operator in the admin app** instead. Verification happens off-system, on the call the operator is already having, which is what \#1069's own "manual admin review for the first iteration" already assumed - the queue only existed to hold information the operator has in front of them, and it brought a claim document, a five-state machine and `disputed`/`superseded` with it.
  - Two of the epic's original premises were wrong rather than outdated, and both were established by checking the code rather than by reading the issue. `organisationId` was believed to be carried by `users` and `biteTrails`; nothing ever wrote it, the organisation dashboard that listed employees by it was permanently empty, and [[issue-1371]] removed the fields. And "no roles, custom claims, or membership checks exist" stopped being true when \#1469 built the admin app, which needed the role model first and delivered most of \#1075 from the other direction.
  - \#1077 shipped on 8 September 2026, the day the epic was rescoped, and it is the first thing here that writes an ownership fact rather than describing one. It also settled the one respect in which the role model was marked provisional: assignment is a field on the restaurant document, so a staff account is scoped by the restaurants its employer holds rather than by a role that narrows `business`, and the three roles stay independent as \#1075 assumed.
  - \#1074 is the other child that shipped, and part of what it shipped is already dead. It added the ownership fields, which \#1077 now writes, and a `RestaurantClaim` model for the flow that is now closed - it never had an importer, and \#1077 removed it. A model added ahead of its writer is cheap; a model added ahead of a _decision_ is what this cost.
  - The role model is settled, and the one respect in which it was marked provisional is settled too. A staff account holds `staff` and not `business`, which forces `roleGuard` to accept a set of roles. That follows from staff being an independent role rather than a narrowing of `business`, and it was recorded as a decision that may move because it depended on the assignment mechanics \#1077 settles. \#1077 settled them the other way from the concern: the assignment is a field on the restaurant, so it constrains nothing about how the roles relate.
  - \#1078 is still the risk. Everything above writes fields and claims that the open Firestore rules do not protect, so until it lands this epic is a client-side gate over an open database - it stops an account reaching a page, not a determined caller writing a document.
