# Recorded Decisions

## Purpose

Every recorded decision in the graph, in one register, so that a decision id can be
resolved without grepping. Per `UF-6` in [[Use Case Format]] a page links the decisions
that bind it rather than embedding them.

## How an id is formed

A decision is cited as **`RD-<REGISTER>-<n>`**.

- **The register prefix is part of the id.** `RD-UR-6` and `RD-VRC-6` are different
  decisions. A bare `RD-6` resolves to nothing and must not be written.
- **The register names the subject the decision binds**, not the page that stores it -
  every decision below is stored here.
- **Numbers are historical.** They need not be contiguous within a register, and a
  number is not reused after its decision is removed.

| Register   | Subject                                                                                                               | Binds                                |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `RD-UR-*`  | Roles, the capability matrix, and their relationship to the personas                                                  | [[User Roles]]                       |
| `RD-VRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate verification                                                                                     | [[UC - Verify Restaurant Candidate]] |
| `RD-DRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate detection                                                                                        | [[UC - Detect Restaurant Candidate]] |
| `RD-GL-*`  | The glossary's terms and the naming rule                                                                              | [[Glossary]]                         |
| `RD-TS-*`  | Guest table sessions and table orders: occupancy, sharing, identity, expiry, the staff queue, and QR abuse protection | [[Table Visit]]                      |

Decisions about **how the specification work is written and tracked** are not system
decisions and are deliberately not here: they are `RD-PD-*`, in
`claude/process-decisions.md` in the project docs.

| `RD-TS-1`  | **A guest scan does not occupy the table. It raises a pending signal that staff confirm.** A QR code establishes a table _context_ and never a presence: the sticker can be photographed, posted, or scanned from the car park. So a scan at a table nobody has seated writes a `pending` session, which cannot order, and the table's live state is not touched at all - the worst a remote scan can do is put one row on a screen in the restaurant. The alternative considered was letting a scan seat the table, refused because it hands anyone holding a photograph of a sticker the power to take a real table out of service                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `RD-TS-2`  | **Confirming is the seating, not a second action.** `transitionTableState` activates a table's pending sessions in the same commit that opens its visit, so a host who seats a party has already admitted every guest who scanned while waiting. | `RD-TS-3`  | **A table session is shared: the second guest to scan joins the first one's visit.** Not enforced by a check but by construction - the join path follows `TableState.visitId`, the single pointer at an open visit, written and dropped by the commit that moves the table's status. | `RD-TS-4`  | **A guest participates through anonymous authentication, and an anonymous account is not a member.** The uid is what `firestore.rules` matches the session document against and what `linkWith*` upgrades in place, so the guest who registers at the end of the meal keeps the session that knows what they ordered. | `RD-TS-8`  | **A menu states its own currency, and absent means "not stated" rather than a default.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1102. `Menu.currency` is ISO 4217, set by the owner in the business editor from a control pre-filled by the device and still requiring a save - so the value is one somebody chose rather than one the product inferred. | `RD-TS-5`  | **A session ends on visit close, or after an idle timeout the restaurant configures.** The timeout is `TableOrderingSettings.sessionIdleTimeoutMinutes`, defaulting to two hours - longer than a meal rather than close to one, because the cost of being too short is a guest whose starter has arrived being told they are no longer at their table. **Expiry is computed, not swept:** `isTableSessionExpired` is a pure predicate and the next callable that observes an expired session persists the status. | `RD-TS-9`  | **An order belongs to one phone; the visit is what is shared.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103, settling the last unsettled half of the sharing question. | `RD-TS-10` | **The guest's phone sends the prices it displayed, and any difference refuses the whole order.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103. There are three ways to keep "the prices on the order are the prices the guest saw" and only this one works. Snapshotting whatever the menu says at submission silently recharges a guest whose dish was repriced while they read its description; trusting the number the client sends lets a client name its own price. So the number is a **claim**, `submitTableOrder` compares it to the live menu, and a mismatch answers `priceChanged` naming the item and both prices. What is written is always the menu's number. The same argument runs for the currency and for availability, which is why all three refusals name the item. A **partial** order - send the lines that are still fine - was refused: three of the four dishes a table asked for is worse than none plus a sentence saying why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `RD-TS-11` | **The guest's order moves the table to `ordering`, and writes that transition itself.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103. `transitionTableState` is guarded by `requireTableStateAuthority`, which admits `staff`, `business` and `admin` and then checks `/restaurantStaff/{uid}` - a guest holds none of those, and widening the guard would hand the floor's state machine to anybody holding a photographed QR code. A table **already** `ordering` is left alone and the order still lands, since refusing that would refuse every round after the first                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `RD-TS-12` | **A guest's status screen shows their own orders, not the party's.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1104. `RD-TS-9` shares the _visit_ and the bill, so the opposite answer was the obvious one and was refused on what a phone can actually prove. A visit-scoped read needs the guest's membership of that visit provable in `firestore.rules`, and the only pointer at it is the session document - whose name is derived from the table the guest **scanned**, while the visit names the table the party is sitting at **now**. A party moved between the two would have the derivation miss. The alternative was recording every guest on the visit as a second copy of what the sessions already say, maintained by two callables, so that a guest could be shown what the person opposite them ordered - which nobody had asked for. | `RD-TS-13` | **The cancellation reason is declared with its reader and written by its writer.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1104. `TableOrder.cancellationReason` is added by the issue that **renders** it, and filled by the staff cancellation of issue \#1105. That is the reverse of the order this repository usually works in, and it is the reverse of `RD-TS-9`'s treatment of `idempotencyKey`, which \#1103 deliberately left absent for \#1108 to add - the distinction being who the field is _for_. It is optional, and an absent one is ordinary rather than a gap: a kitchen mid-rush is not always going to type a sentence, so the screen names the cancellation either way and adds the reason where there is one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `RD-TS-14` | **The staff queue reads one collection-group query, scoped to the restaurant by a field on the order.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1105. Orders hang from the visit (`RD-TS-9`), so tonight's orders are spread over one subcollection per party and there is no path that holds them all. The alternative was a Firestore listener per open visit, attached and detached on every seating and every clearing - a listener churn proportional to the service rather than to the room. So the queue is `collectionGroup('orders')` filtered on `restaurantId`, which `submitTableOrder` already writes on to every order. The rule that admits it has no `{restaurantId}` in its path and therefore reads `resource.data.restaurantId`, which makes the `where` the **permission** rather than a filter: Firestore admits the query only when its constraints prove the condition, so a query without it is refused whole rather than returning every order in BiteTribe. That is `RD-TS-12`'s mechanism pointed at a restaurant instead of at a guest. A second `where` on the status bounds the read to the open orders, because a queue that streamed every order a restaurant had ever taken would grow without limit. Both need a collection-group index exemption, and indexes in this repository deploy by hand                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `RD-TS-15` | **A queue row waits for the backend; the floor plan does not.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1105, and deliberately the opposite of the optimistic layer `RD`-less issue \#1094 built for a table. A table that does not change colour while a party stands in front of the host reads as a tap that missed, and is answered with a second tap; a queue row is under the presser's finger, is answered within a round trip, and an order that appeared to move and then moved back is a kitchen that has already started cooking. So the row shows that it is busy and waits - per order, so one slow call does not freeze the rest of the pass. The same asymmetry decides the idempotency question: a table transition is queued offline and replayed and needs a key naming the intent (issue \#1096), and an order transition is sent while somebody is looking at the screen, where `expectedStatus` already refuses the replay                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `RD-TS-16` | **Cancelling asks for a reason before it sends, and the backend refuses one without.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1105, completing `RD-TS-13`. | `RD-TS-17` | **The busy-service alert is a device preference, off by default, and never the only channel.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1105. | `RD-TS-18` | **A call for a waiter is named after the table and the kind, not generated.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1106. | `RD-TS-20` | **The rate limit is the document name plus one clock, and the clock runs from the request.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1106. | `RD-TS-22` | **An acknowledgement has no `expectedStatus`, and a second press is not a conflict.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1106, and the one place this deliberately differs from `transitionTableOrderStatus` beside it. An order transition has four destinations and two members of staff pressing different ones in the same second is a real disagreement worth refusing; an acknowledgement has exactly one destination, so two people pressing it both wanted what happened and the row has already gone from both screens. The second press is therefore answered with the **stored** values and `changed: false`, so two devices agree on who answered the table and when, rather than with an error about a race that cost nobody anything. It is also why there is no confirmation dialog: a cancellation takes something away from a guest and owes them a sentence (`RD-TS-16`), and an acknowledgement gives them one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `RD-TS-23` | **The two staff lists sort in opposite directions, on one screen, on purpose.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1106. A queue of tickets is worked **newest first**, because the thing that just landed is the thing nobody has read and everything already accepted has somebody working on it (`RD-TS-14`'s screen); a list of people waiting is worked **oldest first**, because the guest who has been waving longest is the one nobody has walked to, and any other order is a room answered unfairly. The repeat tap is what makes that stable: `lastRequestedAt` moves and `requestedAt` does not, so tapping again cannot push a table up the list - the row says "asked again" instead. The urgency threshold differs for the same reason: three minutes for a raised hand against twelve for an order, because an order is waiting on a kitchen that is cooking it and a raised hand is waiting on nobody at all                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `RD-TS-24` | **The scan limit is two counters, cheapest first, and the durable one is the higher.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue \#1107. The in-memory counter of issue \#1100 stays in front of the durable one in `durable-scan-rate-limit.ts`, and the order is the whole cost argument: a flood against one warm instance is absorbed for free after the thirtieth request - no reads, no writes, nothing that grows with how hard somebody is trying - and only traffic spread thinly enough to look ordinary to each instance reaches counters that cost something. The durable limits are therefore deliberately **higher** than the per-instance ones rather than a second opinion on the same requests: they count the aggregate across however many instances are running, and setting them at or below the memory limits would mean the free gate never fired. A third dimension is added, the IP, because `clientOf` answers the uid **or** the address - so an attacker holding one throwaway anonymous account per request is bucketed by neither, and anonymous accounts are free and unlimited by `RD-TS-4`. What that dimension is worth is stated rather than assumed: `X-Forwarded-For` reaches `req.ip`, so it is a cost raiser and not a boundary, and the token and client counts are the two a caller cannot choose. A Firestore failure **admits** the scan, because a limiter that cannot reach its counters and therefore refuses every guest in the product is a worse outage than the one it was reacting to |
| `RD-TS-25` | **An anomaly is a table signal, not a log, and it is raised on the crossing rather than on the refusal.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue \#1107. `/restaurants/{id}/scanAnomalies/{n}_{tableId}_{kind}` takes `RD-TS-18`'s address for `RD-TS-18`'s three reasons: the collection is bounded by the room at one document per table per kind, a repeat joins the row that is up, and a staff screen reads the whole of it with no query, no index and no collection-group rule. The log this replaces would have grown **with the attack**, which is a denial of service wearing the costume of a security feature. The same reasoning bounds the writer twice over: only the request that takes a bucket past its limit raises a row, and a row already raised inside `SCAN_ANOMALY_QUIET_MS` is not written again - so `ScanAnomaly.count` counts **raisings and not requests**, which the row says, and forty thousand resolutions in a minute are one. Nothing reads an anomaly back to decide anything, so a wrong one costs a restaurant a dismissed row rather than a guest a meal, and a dismissal re-opens on the next raising because it says somebody read the row and not that the table is exempt                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `RD-TS-26` | **A pending session is drawn with the staff screens, and is deliberately not an anomaly.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue \#1107, answering the question that issue put to whoever picked it up. `RD-TS-1` says a scan at an unseated table raises a signal staff confirm; issue \#1101 wrote that signal, `firestore.rules` admitted every reader of the floor plan to it, and nothing drew it - so the confirming was asked of a screen that did not show it, and issue \#1106 refused a guest's call for a waiter from a `pending` session while naming this as the reason. It lands as its own list above the tables that are calling, in the warning tone rather than the danger one, grouped one row per **table** rather than per phone - three friends who each scan the code at the door are one party, and three rows would ask a host to seat one table three times. Folding it into the anomaly list would have made the ordinary case look like an incident, which is the reliable way to get a list stopped being read; a guest at the door is a person waiting, and the answer to them is to walk over                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-TS-27` | **The coarse location is a signal on a screen and never a gate, and the coordinates are not stored.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue \#1107. A guest may tick a box on the confirmation screen to share roughly where they are; a guest who does not, whose device has no fix, or whose app has no location grant starts exactly the same session, reads the same menu and places the same order. That is what "never as the sole gate" has to mean in code rather than in prose - a check that is optional but denies something when present is a gate with an opt-out, and the people it would lock out are a basement dining room and an old phone rather than anybody photographing stickers. It **never prompts**: `getCurrentPosition` refuses rather than asking when the grant is undetermined, because the OS prompt belongs to onboarding, which explains what a position is for before spending it. The coordinates are compared to the restaurant's and discarded, and only a rounded distance survives, on the rows far enough away to be worth one - a document holding a guest's latitude would be readable by every member of staff through `readsFloorPlan`, which is a reasonable rule for "table 12 is calling" and an unreasonable one for where somebody was standing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `RD-TS-28` | **Rotation is the restaurant's authority; clearing the row that prompts it is the floor's.** Taken 14 September 2026 on issue \#1107. `dismissScanAnomaly` takes `requireTableStateAuthority`, the same door as seating a table and answering a call, because the rows sit on the screens the floor is already being read from and a second list of who may clear one would be free to disagree with who may answer a table. `rotateTableQrToken` and the new `rotateTableQrTokens` keep `requireRestaurantAuthority`: every other action on those screens moves something on a floor and is undone by moving it back, and this one invalidates a printed sticker and is undone by a trip to the printer. The bulk rotation refuses a call that names **neither** a room nor a set of tables, because "rotate every code in the building" must not be what a caller gets by leaving an argument out; it runs one transaction per table rather than one over the room, because the all-or-nothing guarantee cannot honestly be had at that size and an owner whose run failed at table 19 of 24 is better served by 18 rotated tables and an error than by nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
