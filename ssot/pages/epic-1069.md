- [epic: Restaurant ownership, claiming and authorization](https://github.com/muhammedgaygisiz/travellers-apps/issues/1069) (Issue \#1069)
- Description
  - \# Epic: Restaurant ownership, claiming and authorization
  - Part of \#735 (stage 0).
  - Give restaurants a real owner and give the backend a real authorization model, so that everything built on top of the floor plan can be trusted.
  - \#\# Why this is first
  - The Restaurant Interaction Platform vision assumes restaurants can be claimed. They cannot today.
  - `Restaurant` has no `ownerUserId`, `organisationId`, or claim state (`libs/bite-tribe-common/model/src/lib/restaurant.ts`).
  - No roles, custom claims, or membership checks exist in `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/functions/src`.
  - `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/firestore.rules` allows every authenticated user to read and write every document.
  - Any floor plan, table state, visit, or order written under those rules is writable by any logged-in user. Building stages 1 to 4 first would mean shipping a restaurant operations system with no access control and retrofitting it later, which is strictly harder and riskier.
  - \#\# Goal
  - A restaurant has exactly one accountable owner. Business users can only read and write the restaurants they own. Staff can act on those restaurants within a narrower permission set. Admins can review and resolve claims and disputes.
  - \#\# Scope
  - Ownership and claim fields on the Restaurant model
  - Business roles and organisation membership as verifiable backend identity, not client-side state
  - A claim request flow in the business app
  - An admin review and approval workflow, including contested claims
  - Ownership-scoped Firestore security rules replacing the current open rules
  - Business dashboard scoped to owned restaurants
  - \#\# Success criteria
  - A restaurant cannot be edited by a user who does not own it, proven with emulator rule tests for both allow and deny.
  - The Firestore rules no longer contain a blanket `allow read, write: if request.auth != null` for all documents.
  - A business user can submit a claim for an existing restaurant and see its status.
  - An admin can approve, reject, or revoke a claim, and the effect is visible in the business app without a redeploy.
  - Existing unowned restaurants keep working for read paths in the consumer app and are not orphaned by the migration.
  - Roles are carried in verified backend identity so they cannot be forged from the client.
  - \#\# Out of scope
  - Billing, subscriptions, or paid business tiers
  - Multi-organisation restaurant sharing (one owner per restaurant for now)
  - Automated ownership verification via external registries or phone call-back
  - Consumer-side UI changes beyond what the rules require
  - \#\# Product decisions to confirm
  - Is ownership held by a user or by an organisation? Proposal: by organisation, with the claiming user as the first member, because `users` and `biteTrails` already carry `organisationId`.
  - What evidence is required to approve a claim? Proposal: manual admin review for the first iteration, since volume is low pre-scale.
  - What happens to Bites and menus of a restaurant when ownership changes? Proposal: nothing moves; ownership grants maintenance rights only.
