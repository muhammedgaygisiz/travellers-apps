# Recorded Decisions

## Purpose

Every recorded decision in the graph, in one register, so that a decision id can be
resolved without grepping. Per `UF-6` in [[Use Case Format]] a page links the decisions
that bind it rather than embedding them.

## How an id is formed

A decision is cited as **`RD-<REGISTER>-<n>`**.

- **The register prefix is part of the id.** `RD-UR-6` and `RD-VRC-6` are different
  decisions. A bare `RD-6` resolves to nothing and must not be written.
- **The register names the subject the decision binds**, not the page that stores it -
  every decision below is stored here.
- **Numbers are historical.** They need not be contiguous within a register, and a
  number is not reused after its decision is removed.

| Register   | Subject                                                              | Binds                                |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `RD-UR-*`  | Roles, the capability matrix, and their relationship to the personas | [[User Roles]]                       |
| `RD-VRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate verification                                    | [[UC - Verify Restaurant Candidate]] |
| `RD-DRC-*` | Restaurant-candidate detection                                       | [[UC - Detect Restaurant Candidate]] |
| `RD-GL-*`  | The glossary's terms and the naming rule                             | [[Glossary]]                         |
| `RD-TS-*`  | Guest table sessions: occupancy, sharing, identity and expiry        | [[Table Visit]]                      |

Decisions about **how the specification work is written and tracked** are not system
decisions and are deliberately not here: they are `RD-PD-*`, in
`claude/process-decisions.md` in the project docs.

| `RD-GL-1` | **Place is the Bite-side term; Restaurant is the verified entity.** Every Bite carries a `place`; that place is unmatched, or a Candidate, or verified into a Restaurant. The alternative considered was naming the Bite-side concept _Restaurant_ with three states and calling the record a _verified Restaurant_ - refused, because it renames both a field and an entity to gain nothing the two words do not already carry                                                                                                                  |
| `RD-GL-2` | **"Restaurant profile" is retired as a domain term.** It resolved to three different things: the public restaurant page, the completeness scope of [[UC - Finish Restaurant Profile And Menu Completeness]], and the removed `PublicUser.isRestaurant` account type ([[issue-1371]]). | `RD-GL-3` | **Restaurant claim and Restaurant ownership are two terms, not one.** A claim is the step _before_ ownership - settling who should own a restaurant that has none; ownership is the state that one account is accountable for it. The claim is not a record: \#1076 declined the self-service flow and \#1077 removed the `RestaurantClaim` model, so a claim is settled off-system and reaches BiteTribe as the operator's assignment and its reason. Renaming claim to ownership was considered and refused - it collapses two states into one |
| `RD-GL-4` | **A glossary term is human-readable; a code identifier is an attribute of it.** `RestaurantCandidate` does not contradict _Restaurant candidate_, and `Bucketlist`, `BucketList` and `bucketlistId` do not contradict _Bucket list_. Differences of case or spacing between [[Glossary]] and the code are therefore not drift and are not tracked as findings. Whether the code identifiers should be unified among themselves is a code question, not a glossary one                                                                            |

## `RD-TS-*` - Guest table sessions

Taken 13 September 2026, on implementing issue \#1101. These are the four stage 2 and
stage 3 questions [[epic-1072]] carried as proposals and that issue was blocked on. They
are recorded here rather than left in [[Current State - Open Questions]] because each of
them is now a shape in the database.

| #          | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-TS-1`  | **A guest scan does not occupy the table. It raises a pending signal that staff confirm.** A QR code establishes a table _context_ and never a presence: the sticker can be photographed, posted, or scanned from the car park. So a scan at a table nobody has seated writes a `pending` session, which cannot order, and the table's live state is not touched at all - the worst a remote scan can do is put one row on a screen in the restaurant. The alternative considered was letting a scan seat the table, refused because it hands anyone holding a photograph of a sticker the power to take a real table out of service                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `RD-TS-2`  | **Confirming is the seating, not a second action.** `transitionTableState` activates a table's pending sessions in the same commit that opens its visit, so a host who seats a party has already admitted every guest who scanned while waiting. A per-guest approval queue was considered and refused: it asks the restaurant a question it has already answered by sitting the party down, and it is work during service                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `RD-TS-3`  | **A table session is shared: the second guest to scan joins the first one's visit.** Not enforced by a check but by construction - the join path follows `TableState.visitId`, the single pointer at an open visit, written and dropped by the commit that moves the table's status. This also makes "a guest cannot join a closed visit" true rather than checked: ending the visit dropped the pointer, so a later scan is a new pending session, which is the honest description of a party that came back after the table was cleared                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `RD-TS-4`  | **A guest participates through anonymous authentication, and an anonymous account is not a member.** The uid is what `firestore.rules` matches the session document against and what `linkWith*` upgrades in place, so the guest who registers at the end of the meal keeps the session that knows what they ordered. Two consequences are part of the decision: `createUserOnAuthCreate` writes no `/users` document for a provider-less account, and `authGuard` and `startGuard` ask for a _member_ rather than a session, so a scanned table cannot become a way into the app. A server-issued session secret with no Firebase account was considered and refused - every guest read of their own session would then need a callable, because rules would have no uid to match                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `RD-TS-6`  | **A refusal means there is nothing to show; being unable to order is a different answer.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1102. `tableOrderingDisabled` and `orderingPaused` left `TABLE_SCAN_REFUSAL_REASONS`, which is ten and was twelve, and became {@link TableOrderingAvailability} carried on a scan that _resolved_ - because neither is a reason to withhold a menu: the code is valid, the table is real, and the dishes and prices are what the guest wanted to read. **`restaurantClosed` stayed a refusal**, and the line is deliberate: it is a statement about the _restaurant_ rather than about ordering, a menu under a "closed" heading reads as an invitation, and unlike a pause there is nobody on the premises to correct it                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `RD-TS-7`  | **A menu is public information, served by a callable rather than by a read rule.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1102. A public menu page needs both `/menus/{menuId}` and `/restaurants/{restaurantId}`, and opening the second in `firestore.rules` would publish `ownerUserId`, `claimStatus` and the whole `tableOrdering` configuration. `loadPublicMenu` assembles both halves field by field instead, which is what makes "the public read path exposes the menu only" hold as those documents grow. It takes a **`restaurantId` and not a token**, because the second entry point is a link a restaurant publishes and such a restaurant may have no floor plan and no printed codes at all - a token scopes the _table_ context and never the menu                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `RD-TS-8`  | **A menu states its own currency, and absent means "not stated" rather than a default.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1102. `Menu.currency` is ISO 4217, set by the owner in the business editor from a control pre-filled by the device and still requiring a save - so the value is one somebody chose rather than one the product inferred. On the _menu_ and not the restaurant, because a price is a property of the menu it is written on: a second menu at an airport terminal is priced in that terminal's currency. A menu with none renders bare numbers, which a reader can ask about, rather than the hardcoded euro sign the consumer renderer used to print at every restaurant on earth                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-TS-5`  | **A session ends on visit close, or after an idle timeout the restaurant configures.** The timeout is `TableOrderingSettings.sessionIdleTimeoutMinutes`, defaulting to two hours - longer than a meal rather than close to one, because the cost of being too short is a guest whose starter has arrived being told they are no longer at their table. **Expiry is computed, not swept:** `isTableSessionExpired` is a pure predicate and the next callable that observes an expired session persists the status. No scheduled job exists, because a session nobody touches costs nothing and matters to nobody                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `RD-TS-9`  | **An order belongs to one phone; the visit is what is shared.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103, settling the last unsettled half of the sharing question. One `TableOrder` per submission, under the visit, carrying the `guestUserId` and `sessionId` that sent it - so the party shares a visit and a bill, and "who ordered the second bottle" is answerable without asking every guest to have an account. A **per-line** attribution was considered and refused: a line is built from a cart row that already merges repeated taps, so attributing it would require the cart to stop merging, and a party ordering a round of the same beer would then read as one line per person. A single **table-wide** order was also refused: two phones ordering at once would contend on one document, and the restaurant would lose the grain the kitchen actually uses, which is the ticket                                                  |
| `RD-TS-10` | **The guest's phone sends the prices it displayed, and any difference refuses the whole order.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103. There are three ways to keep "the prices on the order are the prices the guest saw" and only this one works. Snapshotting whatever the menu says at submission silently recharges a guest whose dish was repriced while they read its description; trusting the number the client sends lets a client name its own price. So the number is a **claim**, `submitTableOrder` compares it to the live menu, and a mismatch answers `priceChanged` naming the item and both prices. What is written is always the menu's number. The same argument runs for the currency and for availability, which is why all three refusals name the item. A **partial** order - send the lines that are still fine - was refused: three of the four dishes a table asked for is worse than none plus a sentence saying why |
| `RD-TS-11` | **The guest's order moves the table to `ordering`, and writes that transition itself.** Taken 13 September 2026 on issue \#1103. `transitionTableState` is guarded by `requireTableStateAuthority`, which admits `staff`, `business` and `admin` and then checks `/restaurantStaff/{uid}` - a guest holds none of those, and widening the guard would hand the floor's state machine to anybody holding a photographed QR code. So `submitTableOrder` writes the `occupied -> ordering` change in its own transaction, from the same `TableState` and `TableStateTransition` types and against the same transition matrix; the types are the drift protection, because a field added to either is a compile error in both places. A table **already** `ordering` is left alone and the order still lands, since refusing that would refuse every round after the first                                                                               |

## `RD-VRC-*` - Restaurant candidate verification

Taken 7 September 2026.

| #           | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-VRC-6`  | **Scope.** `V19` and `V23` stay inside the verification transaction but are _referenced_ steps, owned by `UC-GIM` and `UC-ARB`. This Use Case covers verification only; dismissal and duplicate resolution are referenced Use Cases with their own terminal states. Owner assignment was an optional step at `V10`, referencing `UC-ARO`. **Corrected 9 September 2026:** \#1077 delivered it as a separate Operator action in the Admin App, against a Restaurant that already exists and a target that already holds `business`. `V10` is removed; `UC-ARO` is downstream of `END-V4` rather than a step inside this Use Case (`R-15`)                                                                                                                                        |
| `RD-VRC-7`  | **`merged` is removed.** `merged` and `mergedIntoCandidateId` go from both model copies, and `V17`'s merge branch with them. A duplicate Candidate is resolved against an **already verified Restaurant**, which needs no status of its own. Real merging already happens by document-id convergence, which unions `biteIds` without any status, event or audit trail                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `RD-VRC-8`  | **`UC-DRC` owns every producer.** Both the automatic trigger and the Operator's on-demand clustering callable belong to detection, so the threshold divergence is stated once, next to the threshold                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `RD-VRC-9`  | **Refused evidence is not rerouted.** When a write onto a non-`pending` Candidate is refused, the refusal is logged and no Bite is written. Those Bites carry no `restaurantId` and therefore stay visible on the Admin App's Bite-places surface. Detection does not become a writer of `bite.restaurantId`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `RD-VRC-10` | **The on-demand producer keeps no evidence threshold.** A deliberate Operator action is the safeguard. The collection's invariant is restated instead: every Candidate is either backed by at least five matching Bites within 200 m, **or** marked as Operator-created                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `RD-VRC-11` | **The derived-menu marker never clears automatically.** Retraction is an explicit confirmation by the Restaurant Owner, and is sequenced behind owner assignment (`UC-ARO`) rather than shipped alongside the marker — so the marker is never a declared state nothing can reach, which is the mistake `RD-VRC-7` removes. **Premise corrected 9 September 2026:** the sequencing was originally justified by `ownerUserId` having no writer, and \#1077 gave it one. **Corrected again 10 September 2026:** \#1079 scoped the Business App to the assigned restaurants, so the surface a Restaurant Owner would retract from exists. The decision stands on the half that remains — nothing is assigned in production, so there is still no Restaurant Owner who could retract |
| `RD-VRC-16` | **The Candidate list has one server-side order and no selectable ordering:** `evidence.biteCount` descending, then `createdAtTimestamp` descending. The consumer app's client-side sort criterion reorders an already-fetched array, which cannot affect _which_ Candidates a limited query returns — and that is this list's actual defect. A work queue for two Operators also has one defensible default, unlike a discovery feed where preference genuinely varies. Recorded so the divergence from the consumer convention reads as deliberate                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |

## `RD-DRC-*` - Restaurant candidate detection

Taken 7 September 2026.

| #           | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `RD-DRC-18` | **Detection is one Use Case with two entries and one shared kernel,** not two Use Cases. Entry A has no human actor and Entry B is Operator work, which is the argument for splitting; the argument that wins is that both execute the same clustering code, so `R-1`, `R-5`, `R-7`, `R-8`, `R-9` and `R-10` would have to be stated twice and kept in agreement by hand. `K5` is the only step whose behaviour differs by entry, which makes `RD-VRC-10` one gated step instead of a fact duplicated across two pages                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `RD-DRC-19` | **Automatic detection is a mechanism, not an entry.** It has no actor: it runs in the Functions runtime, is callable from no client, and no human is in the loop. `AF-4` reserves the word _actogram_ for a flow containing at least one interaction step, and names a flow without one a mechanism. The page therefore has one entry - the Operator's - and two mechanisms that converge on the shared kernel. This revises the framing of `RD-DRC-18` rather than reversing it: detection remains one Use Case owning both producers, for the reason `RD-DRC-18` gives. `AF-4` also says such a mechanism is written inside the Use Case whose actor causes it, which would place it in `UC-CMB`; it stays here because both producers execute the same clustering code, which is `RD-DRC-18`'s argument. Recorded 8 September 2026 |
