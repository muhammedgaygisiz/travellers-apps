- Description
  - \# Epic: grow bite-tribe-admin into the BiteTribe operations tool
  - The admin app exists. Issue \#1469 created it, deployed it, gated both privileged apps on Firebase Auth custom claims, and built user management as its first operator surface. This epic owns everything that has to happen next for it to be the place BiteTribe operations actually run.
  - \#\# Why this is separate
  - \#1469 answers one question: does an operator-only app exist and can only operators reach it. It does not answer what operations happen in it, and the two need different decisions.
  - Three things are true today and none of them is acceptable for long:
  - **BiteTribe-internal operations still live in the app we hand to restaurants.** The `migrations` page and restaurant-candidate verification sit in `bite-tribe-business`. They are gated on the `business` role, which every restaurant will hold. A restaurant can run our collection-wide migrations and verify restaurant candidates.
  - **The privileged callables do not check who is calling.** `verifyRestaurantCandidate` accepts any authenticated caller. `setUserRoles` and `listUsersWithRoles` require `admin` because they were written after the role existed; nothing older does.
  - **There is no record of what an operator did.** `setUserRoles` writes to the Cloud Functions log and nowhere else. "Who granted this account business access, and when" is answerable only by someone with log access, and only until the retention window closes.
  - \#\# Goal
  - Every BiteTribe-internal operation happens in the admin app, is available only to an operator, and leaves a record of who did it. The business app contains only what a restaurant does to its own data.
  - \#\# Scope
  - \#\#\# Move the operator surfaces out of the business app
  - `migrations` moves to the admin app. `UC - Run Operational Migrations` describes it as running from "business tooling", which stops being true.
  - Restaurant-candidate verification moves with it, including the dashboard list that feeds it.
  - The business app loses both, and its dashboard becomes purely a restaurant's view of its own content.
  - \#\#\# Harden every privileged callable
  - Audit each callable in `apps/bite-tribe-firebase/functions/src/functions` for who may call it, and apply `requireAdmin` where the answer is "an operator".
  - `verifyRestaurantCandidate` is the known case. It is not necessarily the only one.
  - This is independent of \#1078: rules protect documents, `requireAdmin` protects operations.
  - \#\#\# Restaurant claiming, end to end
  - The flow \#1469 was built for: a restaurant calls, an operator verifies the claim, creates the verified restaurant, sets its owner and grants that owner the `business` role in one action.
  - \#1077 owns the backend workflow. This epic owns the operator surface it is driven from.
  - \#1076 is the self-service half in the business app and stays there.
  - \#\#\# An audit trail an operator can read
  - Every privileged action recorded durably: role grants, claim approvals and rejections, candidate verifications, account state changes.
  - Readable in the admin app, not only in Cloud Logging.
  - Attributable: who acted, on what, when, and with what reason where a reason is required.
  - \#\#\# User management beyond roles
  - Search and paging. `listUsersWithRoles` already pages and the client takes the first 200; that is fine for four accounts and wrong at a few hundred.
  - Disable and enable an account.
  - A support view of one account: its Bites, its restaurants, its subscription state.
  - Deleting an account on a user's behalf. `deleteOwnAccount` exists for the user's own path; an operator-initiated deletion is a different decision with legal weight.
  - \#\#\# Room for the roles the platform will need
  - `admin` and `business` are the whole model. Epic \#735 stage 2 assumes restaurant **staff**, who need a narrower permission set than an owner.
  - Business access is global: an account holding `business` can reach the business app, and ownership scoping is what limits it to its own restaurants (\#1079). Whether staff is a role or a per-restaurant membership is undecided.
  - \#\#\# The moderation surface epic \#1284 could not place
  - \#1284 proposes putting the report queue "most plausibly on the existing business `migrations` page rather than a new app", and asks "who is an operator? There is no admin role in the model today". Both are now out of date. The report queue belongs here, and the role exists.
  - \#\# Success criteria
  - No BiteTribe-internal operation is reachable from `bite-tribe-business`.
  - Every callable that performs an operator action rejects a caller without `admin`, proven by a test per callable.
  - An operator can see who granted a role, approved a claim or verified a candidate, from inside the admin app, without log access.
  - An operator can complete the phone-call claim flow end to end in the admin app: verify, create, assign owner, grant role.
  - User management stays usable at a few hundred accounts.
  - The consumer app is unaffected by every change in this epic.
  - \#\# Out of scope
  - Replacing the open Firestore rules. That is \#1078 and keeps its own branch, its own emulator pass and its own rollback plan.
  - The self-service claim request flow in the business app (\#1076).
  - Automated or model-assisted moderation. \#1284 owns the moderation product decisions; this epic owns only where the queue lives.
  - Billing and subscription administration.
  - Any change to what a restaurant can do to its own data.
  - \#\# Product decisions to confirm
  - **Is `admin` one role or several?** A support operator who can read an account and a full operator who can grant roles are different levels of trust. Proposal: one role until a second person needs access, then split.
  - **Does a destructive operator action require a fresh session?** `freshSessionGuard` already exists in `libs/common/ta-firestore` and forces a token refresh before a route activates. Proposal: apply it to deletion and role revocation, not to reads.
  - **Where does the audit trail live?** A Firestore collection is queryable from the app; Cloud Logging is already written to and needs no schema. Proposal: a collection, because the point is that an operator can read it.
  - **Does the admin app need its own auth domain or session policy?** All three apps currently share `bite-tribe.firebaseapp.com` and the same session lifetime.
  - **What happens to a restaurant's data when an operator deletes the owning account?**
  - \#\# Validation
  - Focused Nx targets for each touched project. A Functions test per callable covering the deny case, not only the allow case. Emulator coverage for anything that writes the audit trail. The admin app's E2E suite gains the deny path for each new operator surface, following the pattern `bite-tribe-business-e2e/src/tests/role-gate.spec.ts` established.

- Notes
  - This epic exists because \#1469 delivered an operator app with no operations in it. The gap is deliberate: shipping a new privileged surface and its access control in one change would have left neither reviewable on its own.
  - Two of its scope items are corrections rather than new work. `migrations` and restaurant-candidate verification are BiteTribe-internal operations that have always lived in the business app, and `verifyRestaurantCandidate` has always accepted any authenticated caller. Both predate the admin app; neither was introduced by it.
  - \#1284 was written before an admin role existed. It asks "who is an operator?" and proposes putting its report queue on the business `migrations` page. Both answers changed with \#1469, and that epic should be re-read rather than followed as written.
